What is unusual today

σ is a score. A score of 3 is as rare as a 3σ move of a normal distribution on the history of the measure: about 1 day in 370. The score divides a move by the volatility known before the session, then maps that ratio to its rarity in the history of the group. Here are the scores of 3 or more, and the levels at the edge of their own history. A stock-specific move needs a cap of $10B. Nothing here is a trade signal. It says where to look first. Funding and liquidity

Money markets and the Federal Reserve balance sheet

The policy rates move in steps and the balance sheet once a week, so they are not exceptions. The funding spreads are. Click rows to chart them.
IORB is the ceiling that matters for banks, the ON RRP rate the floor. SOFR above IORB, or a wide 99th percentile, is repo pressure: quarter-ends, tax dates and settlement of coupons.
Net liquidity is the assets of the Fed less the Treasury General Account and the overnight reverse repo: a popular gauge of the liquidity that the Fed supplies, and its link to stock prices is loose. The balance sheet is weekly (Wednesday).

Stock-specific return around events (development sessions)

The mean residual of the style model summed from 5 sessions before the event. After the event (day +1 on) is what a trade could earn. Events on the same date are not independent, so the t is the t-statistic of the mean across event dates, and it treats the dates as independent of each other. The holdout sessions stay out.

Signal long-shorts after costs

Costs are half the spread that the invariance model of Kyle and Obizhaeva gives from volatility and dollar volume, on each name that enters or leaves a leg. Gross of borrow and impact. Capacity is the leg size at which a day's trade in a thin name (10th percentile of dollar volume) is 5% of its volume, the median over the last year. Daily rebalancing is the worst case: a slow signal rebalanced monthly trades far less.
